F3 question 4 -- can a predicate-defined entity-set be an authorization container?

Fifty predicate-defined sets, each one equality on one indexed column of the
entity row -- the cheapest predicate there is. Every number here is therefore a
FLOOR on the mechanism's cost, not an estimate of it.

=== the membership nobody wrote =============================================
entities satisfying one predicate                   100,000
entity_set_member rows for 50 such sets           5,000,000
fact_ancestry rows those edges imply (C1)        84,625,000

=== applying one refresh, timed and rolled back =============================
full refresh of 50 predicate-defined sets, membership only   23.3s

=== the restrictive direction, priced on one row ============================
One UPDATE of entity.country -- an ordinary data write by a principal holding no
authorization permission -- removes the entity from one predicate-defined set and
adds it to another. What that removal has to retract:

median-degree entity     facts        5   fact_ancestry rows to retract (C1)       20
hub entity               facts    1,010   fact_ancestry rows to retract (C1)    4,040

Under C2 the same removal is one row of entity_set_member -- and that is the
whole of the difference. What C2 does NOT fix is which path the change rides:
the write is a data write, so a REVOCATION travels design §9.3's permissive
path, and §9.3's one rule is that the restrictive change propagates on the fast
path. That is a semantic defect and no closure shape repairs it.
